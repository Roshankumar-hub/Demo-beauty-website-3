# Getting Paid in India (₹0 setup, legal and simple)

> **The one rule that matters most:** never tell a client that a payment method, account, or integration "works" until you have personally verified it. Test with ₹1 from your own second account before you send anyone a payment link. Verify — then claim.

---

## 1. What you actually need

| Need | Free solution | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Receive money | UPI (Google Pay / PhonePe / Paytm / your bank app) | ₹0 |
| A shared payment **link** | PhonePe Business payment link, or any UPI app's "request money" link | ₹0 to create; ~1.99% fee on successful payments for gateway links |
| Invoice | Google Docs → PDF (see `invoice-template.md`) | ₹0 |
| Record keeping | A Google Sheet: date, client, amount, UPI reference | ₹0 |
| Business email | A Gmail account created for this business | ₹0 |

**Total setup cost: ₹0. You do not need a company, a GST number, a current account, or a payment gateway to receive ₹799.**

---

## 2. The payment methods, ranked for your situation

### Option A — Direct UPI (simplest, recommended to start)
1. Use your existing UPI app (Google Pay / PhonePe / Paytm) or your bank's app.
2. Share your **UPI ID** with the client (e.g. `yourname@okhdfcbank`).
3. They send ₹400 and share a screenshot; you check your app and confirm.

**Pros:** instant, free, universally understood in India.
**Cons:** it's your personal UPI — consider opening a **separate UPI ID on your business-bank-linked account** later, once money is flowing, so client money doesn't mix with household money.

### Option B — Payment link (looks more professional)
- **PhonePe Business** payment links are published as **₹0 link creation fee** with a **1.99% transaction fee on successful payments**, and work without a website. (Source: [PhonePe Business — Payment Links](https://business.phonepe.com/payment-link).)
- At ₹799, a 1.99% fee is about **₹16**. Acceptable for the professionalism, but you can simply start with direct UPI and save it.

**How to use it well:** create the link, send it with the message from `06-sales-system/sales-messages.md` (Message 7), and don't follow up on the payment more than once.

### Option C — Payment gateway (only later, and only if needed)
Gateways such as Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU and PhonePe PG onboard small businesses/freelancers using PAN and bank details, and non-registered businesses can use them below the GST threshold. Fees are typically ~1.5–3% per transaction. (Sources: [PhonePe Business — payment gateway without GST](https://business.phonepe.com/articles/payment-gateway-without-gst-do-you-need-registration-to-accept-payments), and the general provider pages.)

**Do this later, not now.** You do not need a gateway to receive ₹799 from five photographers, and gateway onboarding can take days.

## 3. What NOT to do

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Don't ask for cash, cheques, or "advance in a friend's account" | Unverifiable, unprofessional, and it destroys trust |
| Don't use someone else's UPI ID or a family member's account | Creates accounting chaos and looks like a scam to the client |
| Don't take 100% upfront before delivering | It's harder to sell and it removes the shared-incentive of the 50/50 split |
| Don't claim you have a "company account" or a "GST invoice" | You don't. Lying about it is the fastest way to lose a client's trust — and it's a legal problem |
| Don't mark a payment as received from a screenshot alone until it appears in your account | Screenshots can be of "processing" transactions |
| Don't chase a ₹799 payment more than twice | Not worth the relationship; a client who won't pay ₹399 isn't someone to chase |
| Don't forget to record it | Ten seconds now saves an hour in March |

## 4. GST, tax and the details you must get right

- **GST registration is not required** below **₹20 lakh** annual turnover for services in most states (₹10 lakh in certain special-category states). Do not charge GST and do not print a GSTIN. Keep "GST: Not applicable — turnover below threshold" on the invoice. (Sources: [IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/gst-on-freelancers), [Khata Billing](https://khatabilling.com/blogs/how-to-create-invoice-without-gst-number-india).)
- **Income tax still applies** to what you earn. This is your income; keep records and file your return.
- **TDS:** if a business client pays you more than **₹50,000** in a financial year for professional services, they must deduct **10% TDS under section 194J** (threshold raised from ₹30,000 to ₹50,000 effective 1 April 2025). Claim it back in your ITR. At ₹799 per client, this will not arise — but know it exists. (Source: [Tax2win](https://tax2win.in/guide/section-194j-under-income-tax-act).)
- **Keep a simple payment ledger.** Columns: `Date · Client · Invoice no. · Amount · Mode · UPI/Bank reference · Balance due · Notes`. One Google Sheet, updated the day the money arrives.
- **Get a CA's advice once you're earning consistently**, and certainly before you cross ₹5 lakh in a year. Two hours with a CA costs less than the mistakes you'd otherwise make.

## 5. Setting up — step by step (45 minutes, ₹0)

Do this **before** your first outreach, so you're never fumbling while a warm client waits.

- [ ] **Step 1.** Create a business Gmail account: `[businessname]@gmail.com`. Use it for everything client-facing.
- [ ] **Step 2.** Turn on **2-step verification** for that account. Non-negotiable.
- [ ] **Step 3.** Confirm your UPI ID: open your UPI app → profile → check your UPI ID. Write it exactly as it appears.
- [ ] **Step 4.** **Test it:** send yourself ₹1 from a second account (or ask a family member). Open the app and confirm the credit appears. **This is the verification — do not skip it.**
- [ ] **Step 5.** Optional: install **PhonePe Business**, register with PAN + bank details, and create a ₹799 test payment link. If onboarding is slow or blocked, go back to Option A. Do not stall your launch over a payment link.
- [ ] **Step 6.** Create your payment ledger sheet with the columns above.
- [ ] **Step 7.** Create an invoice folder: `Invoices/2026/`. Save invoice `2026-001` as a blank template PDF so you only replace names and dates each time.
- [ ] **Step 8.** Draft the WhatsApp payment message (copy from `06-sales-system/sales-messages.md`, Message 7) and save it in your phone's WhatsApp **Quick Replies** or as a note.
- [ ] **Step 9.** Write down your **fee policy** where you can see it: ₹400 advance, ₹399 at handover, refund of the advance if you fail to deliver within 14 days.

## 6. How to agree scope and timeline before starting work

Never start work on a verbal "yes". Confirm four things **in writing** in one single WhatsApp or email message, and ask them to reply "agreed":

> Hi `[Name]`, just confirming what we've agreed, so nothing is unclear later:
>
> 1. **Service:** 7-Day Enquiry Recovery Sprint — up to 15 open enquiries organised into one tracker (status, next action, follow-up date, priority flags, dashboard), plus up to 30 personalised follow-up drafts in your tone, the 7-day workflow, a handover guide + walkthrough, and a Day-14 check-in with a short factual results summary.
> 2. **Not included:** lead generation, any contact with your couples, CRM setup, editing, or any guarantee of bookings or revenue.
> 3. **Price and payment:** ₹799 total — ₹400 now, ₹399 at handover. One revision round free within 5 days of delivery.
> 4. **Timeline:** 7 calendar days from when I receive both your payment and your completed onboarding form. If your input or your approvals take longer, the timeline moves by the same amount.
> 5. **Your part:** fill the onboarding form (about 10 minutes), send 3–5 sample messages, and approve drafts within 48 hours.
>
> Reply "agreed" and I'll send the payment link and the full agreement. 👍

**Why this matters more than a fancy contract:** a short written summary both people actually read prevents 95% of disputes. Attach the full `04-brand-and-sales/customer-agreement.md` as well — but the summary is what they'll remember.

## 7. What if a client doesn't pay the balance?

Rare at ₹399, but be ready:

1. **Day 0:** polite reminder with the invoice attached.
2. **Day 3:** one more reminder, neutral tone, no lecture: *"Hi `[Name]`, just a nudge on the ₹399 balance — the link is `[link]`. Happy to leave it until after your next wedding if that's easier."*
3. **Day 7:** Final message: *"I'll pause here on the balance. If something's wrong with the work, tell me and I'd rather fix it than chase ₹399."*
4. **If they still don't pay:** let it go. Write it off, note it in your ledger, and **do not** publicly complain, and do not withhold anything already delivered. Protecting your reputation is worth more than ₹399.
5. **If it becomes a pattern** with larger amounts later, that's when you introduce a formal payment term (for example a monthly retainer paid in advance) and possibly a lawyer's letter. Not at ₹399.

**Learn from it instead:** ask yourself whether you skipped the written scope confirmation, or whether you ignored an early signal that they weren't serious.

## 8. What you can honestly say once it's verified

**After you have actually completed the setup and tests, you can say:**

> "Payment is by UPI — I'll send you a payment link, or you can pay directly to my UPI ID. ₹400 to start, ₹399 at handover. Invoice the same day."

**You may NOT say (and must not) unless it is literally true:**
- "We accept cards / international payments" (unless you've actually set that up)
- "We're GST registered" (you are not)
- "We have a company account" (you are a sole proprietor/individual)
- "Our payment gateway is integrated" (it is not)

Being accurate about small things is what makes people trust you with bigger ones.
