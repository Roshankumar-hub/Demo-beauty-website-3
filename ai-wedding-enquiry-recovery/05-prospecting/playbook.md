# Prospecting Playbook (₹0, public sources only)

**Goal:** build a list of **40–60 qualified** independent wedding photographers in India, then contact them personally, in batches, without spamming anyone.

---

## 1. What a qualified prospect looks like

Score every prospect. Only contact those scoring **4 or more**.

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Works on weddings (visible wedding work in their feed/site) | +2 |
| Solo or team of 2–6 (not a 15-person brand, not inactive) | +2 |
| A **visible enquiry route**: "DM for bookings", website contact form, WhatsApp button, pricing post | +2 |
| Active in the **last 60 days** (recent posts, recent client work) | +1 |
| Likely **10+ enquiries/month** (city size, activity level, engagement, price bracket) | +1 |
| Belongs to a **Tier-2/Tier-3 city** where competition for paying customers is lower and WhatsApp is the main channel | +1 |
| They publicise packages/prices (means they send quotes → means follow-up volume) | +1 |
| **Negative:** huge studio with a "book a call with our team" widget | −3 |
| **Negative:** no activity in 3+ months, or fully referral-only with no enquiry route | −3 |
| **Negative:** outside India | −5 |

**Target segment:** the photographer who posts reels of real weddings every week, has a Linktree or a simple website, replies to DMs themselves, and clearly has too much going on.

## 2. Where to find them (all free, all public)

### A. Google Maps — best for finding *businesses* with real contact routes

1. Search: `wedding photographer [city]`, `candid wedding photographer [city]`, `wedding photography studio [city]`
2. Filter for **rating 4.0+** and **recent reviews** (a review from last month = an active business)
3. Open each listing → look for a **website** and a **phone/WhatsApp** — only use details **published on the listing itself**
4. Note the review count: 20–150 reviews usually means a steady, real business (not a brand-new startup, not a big brand)

**Cities that tend to work well for this offer** (Tier-2/Tier-3, high wedding volume, lower ad spend): Indore, Bhopal, Nagpur, Jaipur, Lucknow, Kanpur, Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot, Coimbatore, Madurai, Kochi, Thrissur, Mysuru, Mangaluru, Vijayawada, Visakhapatnam, Chandigarh, Ludhiana, Amritsar, Dehradun, Bhubaneswar, Guwahati, Patna, Ranchi, Raipur.

### B. Instagram — best for judging *how they handle enquiries*

Look for accounts that:
- Post wedding work frequently (last post within 2 weeks)
- **Say "DM for bookings" or "DM for details"** ← this is the strongest single signal that enquiries land in DMs
- Post pricing or "dates open for [month]" — an actively selling studio
- Have under ~30k followers (larger accounts usually have staff)

**Hashtags to search:** `#indianweddingphotographer` `#candidweddingindia` `#weddingphotographerindia` `#candidphotographer` `#[city]wedding` `#[city]photographer` `#weddingphotographydelhi` `#bangaloreweddingphotographer` `#keralaweddingphotographer` `#punewedding`
**Also search:** location tags for wedding venues in your target cities (photographers tag venues — venue pages are a goldmine).

**Only use the business account's public contact route.** If they have an email button or a published email, use that. If the only route is a DM, the DM is fine — it's a business contact channel they've chosen to publish.

### C. Search engines — best for websites with contact forms

Search: `wedding photographer [city] packages`, `candid wedding photographer [city] contact`, `wedding photography [city] price list`
Look for sites that have a **contact form**, an **email address in the footer**, or a WhatsApp link. Those are the best outreach routes (photographers themselves say the website form and email are where they pay attention — Instagram DMs get buried).

### D. Public photography communities and Facebook groups

Examples of India-relevant groups (verify membership and rules yourself before posting):
**VIVAH PHOTOS | Indian Wedding Photography**, **Wedding Photography Delhi**, **Candid Wedding Photographers Delhi**, **Odisha Wedding Photographers**, **Photographers Club of Bangalore**, plus city-specific groups.
(Source: [PocketSuite — wedding photographer networking groups](https://pocketsuite.io/post/wedding-photographer-networking-groups/).)

**Rules for groups:** join, participate for at least 5–7 days, answer questions helpfully, and **never** paste a pitch. When someone posts about being overwhelmed by enquiries, answer the question with something genuinely useful and *then* mention what you do — once. Many groups ban promotion entirely; respect that and use the group for learning and relationships instead.

### E. Public business directories and wedding marketplaces

WedMeGood, WeddingWire India and similar sites list vendors, and vendors who **pay for listings** are demonstrably willing to spend money on enquiries — the warmest possible prospect for this offer. Use these directories **only to identify candidates** from their public profiles; find their own website/email from their public business page.

**Do not** copy data out in bulk, and **do not** use a directory's internal messaging if it's restricted. This is a list you build by hand, 20 at a time.

## 3. What information to record (and what never to)

**Record (public business info only):**
- Business name · City · Public website · Public Instagram handle
- The **published** contact route (website form / business email / WhatsApp published on their site)
- Evidence of recent activity (what they posted, when)
- **One personalised observation you noticed yourself**

**Never record or use:**
- Personal phone numbers found in a private context, scraped lists, or guessed email addresses
- Data from private accounts, closed groups' member lists, or exported databases
- Anything you would not be comfortable explaining out loud to that photographer

**File:** `05-prospecting/prospect-tracker.xlsx` (columns are ready; the "Personalised Observation" column is the one that decides your reply rate).

---

## 4. Writing the personalised observation (the hard part)

This single line is the difference between a reply and a block. Spend 90 seconds per prospect on it.

**Good observations (specific, factual, non-creepy):**
- "Your reel from the lake-side wedding last week — the entry shots were lovely."
- "I saw your package page lists two-day coverage; do most of your enquiries ask about that?"
- "Your bio says 'DM for bookings' — I'm curious how you keep track once 40 of those are open."
- "You posted 'Feb dates filling up' — that usually means a rush of enquiries in the same week."

**Bad observations (generic, flattering, or creepy):**
- "Love your work!" *(means nothing)*
- "Your photography is amazing, I checked all 400 of your posts" *(creepy)*
- "I noticed you have 3 children and shoot in Pune" *(personal data — never)*
- Anything that implies you studied private information

**Rule:** compliment **the work**, question **the process**. Never comment on the person.

## 5. Channels, ranked (based on what photographers themselves say)

| Rank | Channel | Why | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Website contact form** | Photographers say the site form is where they pay attention; it's an intentional business channel | Some forms go to a spam folder — follow up by email if you find one published |
| 2 | **Business email published on their site** | Taken more seriously than DMs; easy to forward internally | Keep it short and put the subject line to work |
| 3 | **Instagram DM to a business account** | Instant, casual, normal in India | High volume of noise; your first line must not sound like a pitch; many DMs go unseen |
| 4 | **Facebook page message** | Similar to DM; older studios check it | Slower responses |
| 5 | **WhatsApp (only numbers published as business contacts on their own site/listing)** | Highest open rate in India | Use **only** if they published it for business contact, and keep it to two messages |
| 6 | **Google Maps listing / Q&A** | Unusual and often ignored | Only for finding the business, not for pitching |
| 7 | **Community/group participation** | Builds trust before the pitch | Slow; may be against group rules to pitch |

**Never:** bulk email tools, DM automation, purchased lists, or sending the same text to 50 people in a day.

## 6. Daily workflow (about 90 minutes, days 3–7 of launch)

| Block | Time | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 min | Research 20 new prospects (3 min each) and fill the tracker |
| 2 | 30 min | Send **10 first messages** — each one personalised, one channel only |
| 3 | 20 min | Send follow-ups to anyone whose 4-day wait has passed |
| 4 | 10 min | Update the tracker: dates, responses, next actions |

**Weekly targets (a realistic baseline to plan for):**
- 100–140 prospects researched
- 70–100 first messages sent (spread over the week, never all at once)
- Expect a **reply rate in the single digits** at first — public accounts of outreach to photographers report very poor response to cold DMs (one builder reported 100+ Instagram DMs with zero responses). Do not judge the idea by the first 20 messages. Judge the **channel mix**, and change it if the numbers stay at zero.

**If you get zero replies after 40 messages:** the problem is almost never the idea — it's the channel or the first line. Switch channels (DM → email/website form), and lead with a **question** rather than a description of your service.

## 7. The prospect tracker

Open `05-prospecting/prospect-tracker.xlsx`. Columns and dropdowns are pre-built:

`Business Name · City · Public Website · Public Instagram Profile · Public Business Contact Method · Evidence of Relevant Business Activity · Personalised Observation · Outreach Channel Used · Outreach Date · Response · Interest Level · Next Action · Next Action Date · Status · Notes`

It includes:
- Dropdowns for response, interest level (0–5), next action and status
- **Automatic highlighting** when a follow-up is due
- Red fill for `DO NOT CONTACT`
- A Rules tab with the daily quota

**Update it the same day, every day.** If the tracker is stale, your follow-ups slip, and the follow-ups are the entire product you're selling.

## 8. Ethics rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Public business information only.** No scraping, no bought lists, no guessed addresses.
2. **One follow-up, then stop.** Two messages total per prospect, unless they engage.
3. **Instant, permanent opt-out.** "Not interested" → mark `DO NOT CONTACT` → never contact again, including from another account.
4. **No bulk sending.** Ten personalised messages beat a hundred identical ones.
5. **No fake claims, no fake urgency, no invented results.** If you say "a few photographers I've spoken to", make sure you actually spoke to them.
6. **Identify yourself in every message.** Who you are, what you do, how to make you stop.
7. **Don't pitch in communities that ban it.** Give value first; sell only in direct replies.
8. **Respect cultural and family time.** Avoid messaging during typical wedding-days/wedding-hours (weekends, evenings after 9 pm). Weekday mornings and early afternoons work best for photographers.
