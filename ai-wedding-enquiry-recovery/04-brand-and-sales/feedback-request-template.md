# Feedback & Testimonial Request Templates

**Golden rule:** ask for **honest feedback first** and the testimonial second, as a separate request. Combining them gets you polite nothings. Asking "what could be better?" first gets you information you can actually use.

**Timing:** send the feedback request on **Day 14** (after the check-in, when they've actually used the tracker). Send the testimonial request 2–3 days later, only if their feedback was positive.

---

## 1. Day 14 — honest feedback request (send this first)

> Hi `[Name]`, quick one — it's been two weeks since the handover, so I'd like to hear how it's actually going.
>
> Three questions, answer as briefly (or bluntly) as you like:
> 1. Have you used the tracker? If yes, roughly how often?
> 2. Did you send any follow-ups that you probably wouldn't have sent before? Which ones?
> 3. What's the most annoying or useless part of it?
>
> Blunt answers help me more than kind ones. Also, if you have two minutes, roughly how many replies, calls or bookings have come from those enquiries since we started? I'll put only your numbers in the summary — nothing invented.

**What you do with the answers:** fill in the Results Summary with their words and numbers, verbatim. If they say "I used it twice and forgot after that", write that down and learn from it. That is the data that makes client #2 better.

---

## 2. Testimonial request (only if feedback was positive)

> Hi `[Name]`, thanks again for the feedback — it's genuinely useful.
>
> Would you be comfortable giving me a short line I can show other photographers? Totally fine to say no.
>
> If yes, anything like two sentences on one of these is perfect:
> • What problem it solved for you
> • What changed in your week
> • Whether you'd recommend it, and to whom
>
> Things I'd be grateful if you *don't* write: results I can't verify (like "booked 5 weddings"), because I don't want to show anyone an unproven number. Honest and specific beats impressive.

**Then ask the permission question explicitly:**

> One more thing — can I use it with your name and studio, or would you prefer it anonymised (e.g. "a wedding photographer in Indore")? Either is completely fine, and I'll only use the version you choose.

---

## 3. If they say yes — confirm in writing (keep this message)

> Thank you. To be clear about how I'll use it: your words exactly as you wrote them, credited as `[name / studio]`, on my website and in proposals to other photographers. No edits to the meaning, no added claims. If you ever want it removed, one message and it's gone within 24 hours. Is that okay?

**Store their confirmation with the testimonial.** If someone later asks to withdraw it, remove it the same day, from everywhere.

---

## 4. Referral request (one gentle ask, 2–3 days after positive feedback)

> Hi `[Name]`, one small favour — do you know one or two photographers who have the same "too many chats, no system" problem? If you introduce me, I'll give them the same ₹799 pilot price and look after them properly.
>
> No pressure at all, and please don't share my number anywhere public — a direct intro is all I need.

**Why one intro beats ten cold DMs:** a referral comes with trust already attached, which is the single hardest thing for a new service to buy.

---

## 5. The negative-feedback script (this is the important one)

If the client is unhappy, do **not** get defensive and do **not** disappear:

> Thank you for telling me straight — that's what I need to hear. Let me make sure I've understood: `[repeat their complaint in your own words]`. Is that right?
>
> Here's what I can do: `[one concrete fix, with a date]`. If that doesn't sort it out, I'll `[refund / partial refund / additional work at no charge]`.
>
> I'd rather fix this properly than pretend it went well.

**Then actually fix it, and follow up once to confirm it's fixed.**

### Refund guidance for a ₹799 service

| Situation | Fair response |
|---|---|
| You didn't deliver the agreed deliverables | Full refund, no argument |
| Delivered late because of you, everything else fine | Partial refund (₹200–₹400) or free extension |
| Drafts were poor quality and one revision round didn't fix it | Full refund, keep the tracker as goodwill |
| They changed their mind after delivery | No refund. Offer to help them use what they got |
| They expected bookings or leads | No refund. Re-explain the scope in writing — this was disclosed in the proposal and the agreement |

**Never argue about ₹799.** A refund costs less than a bad review, and in a referral-driven community, your reputation with 20 photographers is worth far more than one fee.

---

## 6. The "don't ask" list

Do not request a testimonial if:
- The client's feedback was negative or lukewarm (fix it instead)
- You didn't complete the deliverables
- They asked to be left alone
- You would have to imply results you can't verify

**Never:** write the testimonial yourself and ask them to "just approve it", buy reviews, or use a fictional client name. Ever.
