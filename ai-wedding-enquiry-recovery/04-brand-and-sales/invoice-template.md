# Invoice Template (non-GST, small service provider)

**What this is:** a simple, legal invoice format for an unregistered service provider in India. As a service provider **below ₹20 lakh annual turnover**, GST registration is not required, and you must **not** print a GSTIN, HSN/SAC code, or any GST amount. This is called a plain invoice / bill.

> Not tax advice. Verify with a CA once money is flowing. **Never** claim a payment, GST number or account exists until you have actually set it up and verified it.

**How to use:** copy into a Google Doc → replace `[placeholders]` → **File → Download → PDF** → send by email or WhatsApp with the invoice number in the message. Number invoices sequentially: `2026-001`, `2026-002`, …

## Practical rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Invoice number must be unique and sequential** (`2026-001`, `2026-002`) | It is the basic requirement for a valid invoice and it makes your records auditable |
| **Never show a GSTIN you don't have** | Showing a GST number you are not registered for is a serious problem |
| **Never mark a payment received before it is credited in your account** | Any claim of a working payment setup must be true and verified |
| **Keep every invoice as a PDF in one folder** `Invoices/2026/` | You will need them at tax time |
| **Record each payment** — date, amount, UPI reference, client | Ten seconds now saves an hour in March |
| **If a client insists on a "GST invoice"** | Explain plainly that you are below the threshold and cannot charge GST; if they need a GST invoice, this is not a fit for you yet |
| **If a business client deducts TDS** | At ₹799, no TDS applies (10% under section 194J only kicks in above ₹50,000 paid to you in a financial year). If you grow past that, keep Form 16A and claim it in your income tax return |
