# Objection Handling

The 20 objections you will actually hear, what each one really means, and what to say.

**The mindset:** an objection is not a rejection — it is a question the person hasn't asked clearly yet. And some objections are **correct**. When they are, agree, say so, and stop selling. Being the person who talked someone *out* of buying is how you get referrals in a small community.

**Rules for every objection:**
1. Don't argue. Ask one question first.
2. Never invent a result or a discount.
3. If the objection is "I don't have this problem", believe them and leave.
4. Never create urgency that isn't real.
5. Once you've answered, ask a small, easy next step — not "so, shall we start?"

---

## 1. "I don't have that problem, I reply to everyone"

**Really means:** either it's true, or they've never measured it. Most people can't tell the difference.

> That's entirely possible — plenty of photographers run a tight ship.
>
> One honest way to check, takes 30 seconds: search your WhatsApp for any couple who got your price list more than 10 days ago and hasn't replied since. If that list is empty, you genuinely don't need me and I'd rather say so than take your money.

**If they come back with "there are 4 of them":** you have the problem confirmed *by them*, and the sale is nearly done. **If they come back with "there's one, maybe"** → that's a no. Leave it warmly: they'll remember you.

---

## 2. "₹799 for a spreadsheet? I can make a sheet myself"

**Really means:** they're pricing the artefact, not the work.

> You're right that the sheet itself isn't worth ₹799 — it's a template.
>
> What you're paying for is the 8–10 hours of work: reading back through your chats, working out where each enquiry actually stands, deciding the next move for each one, and writing 30 messages that sound like you rather than like a template.
>
> If you'd rather do it yourself, honestly, do — it works fine. Here's a free blank version with the columns and formulas already built: `[link]`. Use it with my compliments.

**Why this wins either way:** the person who takes the free sheet and does the work will remember you when they eventually want help. The person who realises they'll never get around to it comes back.

---

## 3. "Are you a photographer? What do you know about weddings?"

**Really means:** a trust gap. Fair question.

> I'm not a photographer, and I won't pretend to be. You know weddings; I'm not going to teach you about them.
>
> I'm the person who does the organising and drafting work: sorting enquiries, deciding the next action, writing the messages. Every draft is something you approve or throw away — you keep the judgement, I do the typing.

---

## 4. "Why so cheap? Is this some AI junk?"

**Really means:** they're suspicious of low price + AI in the name. Very reasonable.

> Fair question. Two honest answers.
>
> **On the price:** it's a pilot. You'd be one of my first clients, so I'm pricing for feedback and a case study, not for profit. The first three or four clients get it at ₹799. After that it goes up.
>
> **On the AI:** I use AI to help me draft faster, and then I personally review every message. Nothing gets generated and sent by a machine — you read and approve every single draft before it reaches anyone. And I never send anything to your couples. Ever.

---

## 5. "I already have a CRM" (Studio Ninja / Dubsado / HoneyBook)

**Really means:** they bought the tool; the habit may not exist.

> That's good — most photographers I speak to don't have anything.
>
> One question, and I'll take your answer seriously: if you open your CRM right now, do you know the next action for the five longest-waiting enquiries? If the answer is yes, you don't need me at all.
>
> If the CRM has the data but the follow-ups still aren't happening, the problem isn't software — it's the habit and the drafting. That part I can fix, and it works alongside whatever CRM you already pay for.

---

## 6. "I don't want to share my clients' details"

**Really means:** legitimate privacy concern — and you should respect it fully.

> Completely reasonable, and I designed this around exactly that concern.
>
> I ask for initials or first names only — **no phone numbers, no emails, no couple documents**. I work inside a folder that you own, on your Google account. I never contact your clients. Anything on my side is deleted within 30 days, and I confirm it in writing. If you want it deleted sooner, one message and it's done.
>
> Here's the privacy section of the agreement — read it before you decide, not after.

---

## 7. "I'll do it after the season"

**Really means:** usually a polite no. Occasionally it's genuinely true.

> That makes sense — the season isn't the time to add work.
>
> Can I ask what month it slows down for you? I'll message you once then, and not before.
>
> One thing you can do today for free, in two minutes: pick any couple who got your quote more than 10 days ago and never replied, and send them one line asking whether the dates still work. That is the whole idea in miniature. If it feels useful, I'll be here in `[month]`.

---

## 8. "How do I know it will work?"

**Really means:** "prove it". You can't prove results. Be honest.

> I can't prove it will work for you, and I won't pretend to.
>
> What I can show you: the sample tracker and the sample report. Right now I'm a new service with no testimonials — I'd rather tell you that than invent one. What I promise is specific and small: 15 enquiries organised with a next action for each, 30 drafts in your voice, delivered in 7 days, and a full refund of your advance if I fail to deliver.

---

## 9. "Send it to me and I'll have a look"

**Really means:** not now. This is the most common soft no.

> Sure — here's the sample: `[link]`. It's fictional data so you can see the whole thing.
>
> I'll check back in four days to hear what you thought. If it's a no, that's fine — just tell me then and I'll close the file on my side.

**Then: one follow-up, on day 4. Nothing more.**

---

## 10. "Can you do it for free and I'll pay if it works?"

**Really means:** they're transferring the risk to you.

> I understand the instinct, but I don't do that — a pilot needs to be a real transaction for it to be a real test on both sides.
>
> What I can offer instead: a **free 15-minute Enquiry Health Check**. Send me your numbers and I'll tell you honestly what I'd prioritise, with no obligation. If it looks like I'd be useful, the sprint is ₹799.

**The free health check is your only free step.** It costs you 15 minutes and it converts well — but never deliver the full ₹799 scope for free.

---

## 11. "I don't have time to give you all this information"

**Really means:** legitimately busy, or worried about effort.

> That's exactly why I ask for only two things: your price list, and three messages you've already sent.
>
> For the enquiries, do the lazy version: screenshot the chats and send them. I'll do the typing, clean up the list, and send it back to you to check. Your total time is about 10 minutes.

---

## 12. "I'll just forward you my enquiries and you message them"

**Really means:** they want the labour gone. Do not concede this — it breaks the design.

> I understand why that would be easier, but I don't message anyone on your behalf.
>
> Two reasons: your couples contacted you, and a message from an unknown number usually gets ignored — so it would hurt your chances rather than help. And sending from a third party runs into platform and privacy rules that I'm not willing to blur.
>
> What I'll do: write every message so it takes you 30 seconds to send. Copy. Paste. Send.

---

## 13. "I can find a freelancer for ₹200"

**Really means:** competing on price. Don't.

> Probably true — you can find someone cheaper.
>
> Two things worth considering, though. One, I'm not selling you a technical task — I'm reading back through your actual enquiries and writing in your voice, which needs a bit of judgement. Two, everything is in writing: scope, dates, privacy terms, and a full refund if I don't deliver. If you'd rather try the cheaper option first, that's a completely reasonable call.

---

## 14. "My team handles this"

**Really means:** sometimes true, sometimes a brush-off.

> That's helpful to know. Who on your team owns it — and do they have a way to see which enquiries have been waiting longest?
>
> If they do, you genuinely don't need this. If the honest answer is "no one really owns it" or "it's between three of us", that's usually where enquiries fall through the gaps.

---

## 15. "I've paid for leads before and it was a waste of money"

**Really means:** they've been burned, and they're expecting the same from you. This is your warmest prospect if handled honestly.

> I've heard that a lot — I know photographers who paid ₹50,000+ for a listing and converted almost nothing.
>
> I don't sell leads. I don't bring you new enquiries, I don't touch ads, and I can't promise any extra bookings. What I do is organise the enquiries you already have and write the follow-ups you don't have time to write. That's it.
>
> After what you've been through, I'd understand completely if you never spend another rupee on this area. If you'd like to see whether your existing enquiries have anything recoverable in them, I'll show you the sample and you can judge.

---

## 16. "Why shouldn't I just do it with ChatGPT myself?"

**Really means:** they're technical and value-conscious.

> You could — and if you enjoy it, you should.
>
> The reason most people don't: it takes a few hours to set up properly, then it needs doing again every week, and the AI doesn't know your packages or your tone, so the drafts come out generic until you teach it. Also, it has no idea which of your enquiries have been waiting 30 days.
>
> What you're buying from me is the sorting work, the domain knowledge of your packages, your voice, and the fact that it's actually done in 7 days instead of sitting on your list for two months.

---

## 17. "Can I pay after the delivery?"

**Really means:** either distrust, or cash flow. Usually fine with a compromise... but be careful.

> The advance is what makes the 7-day promise possible — it books the slot.
>
> Where I'm flexible: pay ₹400 at the midpoint instead of the start, once you've seen the tracker. Or if a referral you trust introduced us, we can do payment on delivery. What I can't do is start with no commitment at all.

---

## 18. "Give me a discount"

**Really means:** testing the price (sometimes cultural — bargaining is normal in India).

> ₹799 is already the pilot price — it's about a tenth of what a CRM setup costs and less than a tenth of one booking. I'm not building in a margin to discount from.
>
> What I can do: if you have more than 15 enquiries, we can talk about the whole batch in one go. Or if you'd rather start smaller, I can do a ₹399 version: 8 enquiries, 15 drafts, tracker and workflow included.

**Having a smaller, cheaper option is far better than discounting your main offer.** Discounting tells the client the price was fake.

---

## 19. "Send the payment link" — then they go silent

**Really means:** something changed: budget, timing, or a family conversation.

> Hi `[Name]`, no pressure — just checking whether you're still considering it, or whether something changed.
>
> If price is the issue, tell me and I'll be honest about what's possible. If it's timing, tell me the month and I'll come back then. If you've decided against it, that's completely fine too — I'd just rather know than keep wondering.

**Then: stop after this.** If they come back later, treat it as a fresh conversation.

---

## 20. "Are you registered? Do you have a GST number?"

**Really means:** they may need a GST invoice for their accounts.

> Honest answer: I'm not GST-registered, because my turnover is well below the ₹20 lakh threshold for service providers, so no GST is charged on my invoice. I'll send a proper invoice with all the details — amount, date, description, and my PAN on request — which is exactly what's required at this size.
>
> If your accountant needs a GST invoice specifically, then I'm not the right fit yet, and I don't want to make that your problem.

---

## The three things to say when you don't know the answer

1. **"I don't know — let me check and come back to you today."** (Then do, the same day.)
2. **"That's outside what I do. Here's who might help."**
3. **"Based on what you've told me, I don't think you need this. Save your money."**

Number 3 is the one that builds a business.

## After every objection — the internal debrief

Write one line in `01-research-and-validation/validation-experiment.md`'s weekly summary:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Objection heard | "I already have a CRM" |
| What I tried | Asked about their 5 oldest enquiries |
| What happened | Admitted it wasn't updated, but didn't buy |
| What I'll change | Lead next time with the question, not the pitch |

Ten of these, and you will know more about this market than most people selling into it. **If the same objection appears five times and your answer never works, the offer is wrong — not the wording.**
