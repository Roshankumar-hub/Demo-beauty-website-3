# Client Message Templates (12 ready-to-send follow-ups)

For wedding photographers to send to couples in India. Written to sound like a professional Indian photography studio, not a robot and not a salesperson.

**Every template has:** when to use it, when NOT to use it, the copy-paste text, and a WhatsApp-length version where useful.

---

## The 8 rules of these messages (read before sending anything)

1. **Personalise the first line, always.** Mention their event date, their venue, or something they actually said. If a message could be sent to anyone, it will be read like it was sent to everyone.
2. **One question per message. Maximum.** Two questions get zero answers.
3. **Never write "just checking in"** or "gentle reminder" as the whole message. Give them something new: a similar wedding, availability, an answer.
4. **No pressure, no fake urgency.** Never invent "only one date left this season" unless it is literally true. Fake scarcity is the fastest way to lose a referral-driven business.
5. **Hard cap: 3 follow-ups after your first reply, per enquiry.** Then stop. Silence is an answer.
6. **Stop instantly** if they decline, say "not interested", ask you to stop, or block you. Mark the enquiry `Closed - not proceeding`. Never message that number again.
7. **No double messages in a day.** Not on WhatsApp and Instagram together.
8. **Prices in writing only.** Never quote a different price in a follow-up from the one in your price list — that is how trust dies.

**Placeholders used below:** `[Name]`, `[Event date]`, `[Venue/City]`, `[Service]`, `[Your name]`, `[Studio]`, `[Amount]`, `[Link]`, `[Package detail]`.

---

## 1. New enquiry — first reply (send within 24 hours, ideally same day)

**Use when:** someone enquires for the first time, on any channel. This is the highest-value message you will ever send.
**Do not use when:** the enquiry is clearly spam, a vendor pitch, or outside your service area — reply with a one-line polite no instead.

> Hi [Name], thank you for reaching out about your wedding on [Event date] at [Venue/City] — congratulations!
>
> I'd love to understand what you're looking for before I send pricing:
> 1. Is the wedding [number] days, or just the main day?
> 2. Roughly how many people are you expecting?
> 3. Are you looking for photography only, or photo + video?
>
> Once I know these, I'll share the packages that actually fit — no point sending you a 15-page list. I'm [Your name] from [Studio], and I shoot [Service].

**If you already know their answers** (they told you in the enquiry), replace the questions with:
> Since you mentioned [what they said], the package that fits best is [package name] at [Amount]. Would you like me to send the full details and a few similar weddings?

**WhatsApp version (short):**
> Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out about [Event date] at [Venue/City]! Quick question so I can send the right details — is it just the wedding day, or [number] functions? And photography only, or photo + video too? — [Your name], [Studio]

## 2. They received a quote and went quiet (3–4 days later)

**Use when:** you sent a price list or quote, they did not reply for 3+ days, and your price is not the obvious blocker.
**Do not use when:** you have sent this twice already, or they told you the budget doesn't match. In that case use template 7 or 8.

> Hi [Name], hope the wedding planning is going well.
>
> Just so you have a clearer picture — here's a wedding I shot at [similar venue/city] in [month]: [Link]. Similar scale to what you described, so you can see how the final set and the film turn out.
>
> If the [package name] pricing is more than you had planned, tell me honestly — sometimes we can adjust the coverage (for example, [one-day instead of two] or photography without video) instead of losing the date. Would that be useful?

**Why this works:** it gives them something new (a real reference wedding) and it lowers the barrier to saying "your price is too high", which is the information you actually need.

**WhatsApp version:**
> Hi [Name] — sharing two similar weddings I shot: [Link]. If the package price is more than you'd planned, tell me honestly and I'll tell you what we can adjust. No pressure either way. — [Your name]

---

## 3. They requested a call — confirming the slot

**Use when:** they asked to speak.
**Do not use when:** they never asked for a call. Never push a call on someone who only asked for prices — it feels like a sales tactic.

> Hi [Name], happy to talk it through. I'm free:
> • [Day, Date] — [time] to [time]
> • [Day, Date] — [time] to [time]
>
> Send me a time that works and I'll call you on [your number] — or if you prefer, I can send a voice note instead. Before we speak, is there anything specific you want me to have ready (packages, the album samples, travel charges)?

**After the call, always send a written recap:**
> Hi [Name], thanks for the call today. Quick recap of what we discussed:
> • Coverage: [detail]
> • Package: [name] — [Amount]
> • What happens next: [next step]
> If I've got anything wrong, correct me. Otherwise, whenever you're ready, we can block [Event date] for [number] days while you decide.

**WhatsApp version:**
> Hi [Name], happy to talk! I'm free [Day] after [time] or [Day] evening. Which suits you? — [Your name]

---

## 4. They asked for information and never replied (first nudge, 3–4 days later)

**Use when:** you answered their question, they read it (or didn't), and there's been silence for 3–4 days.
**Do not use when:** it has already been used once on this enquiry. Then wait a week and use template 7.

> Hi [Name], you asked about [their exact question] — I replied on [day], in case it got buried in your DMs.
>
> Short version: [one-line answer]. Full details here: [Link].
>
> If [Event date] is still on, let me know and I'll check availability. If your plans have changed, that's completely fine too — just tell me and I won't keep following up.

**Note the last line.** Giving them an easy exit gets you a *reply*, and a reply is worth more than a maybe. It also keeps you on the right side of consent.

**WhatsApp version:**
> Hi [Name], my reply about [topic] may have got buried — short answer: [one line]. Details: [Link]. If the date is still on, tell me and I'll check availability. If plans changed, all good, just let me know so I don't keep messaging you. — [Your name]

---

## 5. Clarifying a package (album, travel, hours, extra days)

**Use when:** they asked a specific question about inclusions. Answer it completely and precisely — vague answers are why enquiries die.
**Do not use when:** the answer is "it depends on a lot of things" — that is not an answer. Give a range and one example.

> Hi [Name], good question — here it is properly:
>
> **Album:** [included / not included / available at ₹X for Y pages]
> **Travel & stay:** [included within X km / charged at actuals for outstation / our team's stay is on us for venues in Y]
> **Hours:** [X hours coverage, extra hours at ₹Y per hour]
> **Delivery:** [photos in X weeks, film in Y weeks]
> **Payment:** [X% to block the date, balance on delivery]
>
> Anything else you want clarified before deciding? Happy to answer all of it in one go so nothing is guesswork.

**WhatsApp version:**
> Hi [Name], answering all of it: Album — [detail]. Travel — [detail]. Hours — [detail]. Delivery — [detail]. Booking — [X]% to block the date. Anything else, ask me. — [Your name]

---

## 6. "We're still deciding" (low-pressure, useful reply)

**Use when:** they said they are deciding, comparing photographers, or waiting on family.
**Do not use when:** they said no. Do not argue with a decision.

> Hi [Name], completely understand — it's a big decision and you should take your time.
>
> Two things that might help:
> • Here's a wedding similar to yours in scale and budget: [Link] — this is the honest version of what your album and film would look like.
> • [Event date] is [available / holding / already booked]. If you want it, I can hold it for [X] days without any payment, so it doesn't get taken while you decide.
>
> Whatever you choose, I hope the wedding is exactly what you're dreaming of.

**If the date is already booked, say so plainly:**
> [Event date] is booked with another family, so I'd rather tell you now than waste your time. If you have an alternate date, I'm happy to check that instead.

**WhatsApp version:**
> Hi [Name], take your time — it's a big call. [Event date] is [status]; I can hold it for [X] days with no payment while you decide. Here's a similar wedding if it helps: [Link]. Whatever you pick, wishing you the best. — [Your name]

---

## 7. Polite final follow-up (the last message you ever send on this enquiry)

**Use when:** 2 follow-ups already sent, still silent, and you want a clean, respectful end. Send it once, then stop and mark the enquiry `Dormant - no reply` (and close it if another week passes).
**Do not use when:** they asked you to stop. Then send nothing — mark it closed.

> Hi [Name], this is my last message about [Event date] — I don't want to keep filling your inbox.
>
> If you've found someone else, no hard feelings at all, and I'd genuinely love to see the photos later. If you're still looking and the date is open on your side, just reply "yes" and I'll send everything you need in one message. If I don't hear back, I'll assume the plans changed.

**WhatsApp version:**
> Hi [Name], last message from me on this so I don't keep disturbing you. If you've gone ahead with someone else, all good — I mean that. If you're still looking, reply "yes" and I'll send everything at once. — [Your name]

**After this:** nothing. Zero messages. If they come back six months later, welcome them warmly.

## 8. They declined (leave the door open, no guilt)

**Use when:** they chose someone else, postponed, or said no.
**Do not use when:** you feel tempted to add "if you change your mind" *twice* in the message. Once is enough.

> Hi [Name], thank you for telling me — genuinely appreciated, most people don't.
>
> I hope [photographer/plans] works out beautifully for you. If anything changes with the date or the coverage, you're always welcome to message me. And if it's useful, I can share [an album vendor / venue contact / a tip] — happy to help even if the booking isn't with me.
>
> Wishing you both a wonderful wedding, [Name].

**Why this is not a waste of time:** in a referral-driven industry, the graceful exit *is* marketing. Public evidence shows photographers live largely on referrals and word of mouth.

**WhatsApp version:**
> Thanks for letting me know, [Name] — genuinely appreciated. Wishing you a beautiful wedding. If anything changes, my door's open. — [Your name]

---

## 9. Booking confirmed — what happens next (bonus, reduces anxiety and refunds)

**Use when:** advance received and the date is blocked. Send within 24 hours.

> Hi [Name], your date is blocked — welcome aboard! Here's exactly what happens now:
>
> 1. **Now:** I'll add your wedding to the calendar as [Event date], [Venue/City].
> 2. **Before the wedding:** I'll share a short shoot-planning checklist [X weeks] before, and we'll talk about which family groups matter most to you.
> 3. **Payment:** [Amount] received, [Amount] due [when].
> 4. **Delivery:** sneak peeks within [X] days, full photos in [X] weeks, film in [X] weeks.
>
> One small request: if you have a Pinterest board or a folder of photos you love, send it across. It helps me shoot *your* taste, not my favourite style.

---

## 10. Stay in touch (past enquiries, future dates) — seasonal only

**Use when:** they declined or postponed earlier, and a relevant season is genuinely approaching (for example, they mentioned a 2027 wedding). **One message, once or twice a year. Never more.**
**Do not use when:** they asked you not to contact them again. Then never.

> Hi [Name], hope you're both doing well. You'd mentioned a possible 2027 wedding — my [month] dates are opening up now. If you're still planning, tell me the dates and I'll check availability for you. If not, ignore this and I won't follow up again.

**Note:** this is a business-to-customer message from a studio the couple already contacted. Keep it to one, keep it useful, and never make it a "festival offer" broadcast.

---

## 11. Call no-show or reschedule

**Use when:** they missed a scheduled call. Assume good faith — no sarcasm, no guilt-tripping.

> Hi [Name], we had a call planned for [day/time] — no problem at all, these weeks are chaotic.
>
> Want to move it to [new option 1] or [new option 2]? Or if a call is difficult, I can send everything in writing and you can reply whenever you have five minutes.

---

## 12. The "send me your budget" reply (dealing with price-only enquiries)

**Use when:** their first message is only "what's your price?" with no other details.
**Do not use when:** you have a public price list. Then just send the price list — no games.

> Hi [Name], happy to share — my packages start at [Amount] for [what that includes], and most [city] weddings at your scale land between [X] and [Y] depending on [days + photo/video + album].
>
> If you tell me your rough budget range, I'll tell you honestly whether I can fit it, and what I'd recommend at that level. If my number is above it, I'd rather say so on day one than waste your time.

---

## Quality checklist before sending any message

- [ ] Their name is correct (spelled right)
- [ ] The first line mentions something specific to them
- [ ] Exactly one question (or zero, if it's a closing message)
- [ ] No "just checking in", no "gentle reminder"
- [ ] No invented scarcity or pressure
- [ ] No price that contradicts the price list
- [ ] Plain, warm, and short enough to read on a phone
- [ ] If it's a follow-up: is this within the 3-follow-up cap?
- [ ] If they said no or stop: am I sending nothing at all? (correct answer: yes)
