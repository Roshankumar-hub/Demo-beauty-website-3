# Validation Experiment (₹0)

**Goal:** Find out whether independent Indian wedding photographers have a real, *expensive-to-them* enquiry-tracking problem — and whether at least one will pay ₹799 to have it solved.
**Budget:** ₹0
**Time:** 14 days, roughly 1.5–2.5 hours a day
**Success at the end:** at least 1 paid Sprint (₹400 advance received). Anything else is learning, not failure — but it is also not validation.

---

## 1. The hypothesis

> **H1:** At least 5 out of 20 wedding photographers can describe, with specifics (dates, counts, what happened), enquiries that went unanswered or unfollowed, and say that this bothers them.
>
> **H2:** At least 1 out of those 20 will pay ₹799 in advance for a 7-day service that fixes it.

H1 is about the problem. H2 is about willingness to pay. **They are different tests. Passing H1 and failing H2 is the most likely outcome and it is still useful information.**

---

## 2. The free experiment design

Two tracks, run in parallel. Track A builds credibility. Track B makes money.

### Track A — Discovery conversations (free, no selling)

- **Who:** 12–20 photographers matching the profile in §3.
- **Ask:** a 10–15 minute chat. Say clearly: *"I'm researching how photographers handle enquiries. I'm not selling anything in this chat."* Then actually not sell.
- **Where:** a chat on WhatsApp/Instagram where they engaged first, a phone call, or a plain email exchange. Zoom is optional.
- **Cost:** ₹0.
- **Output:** a filled row in `interview-log.md` and an honest score in §6.

### Track B — Paid pilot offer (the real test)

- **Who:** everyone from Track A who scored **4 or 5** in §6, plus direct outreach to qualified prospects (see `05-prospecting/playbook.md`).
- **Ask:** exactly one thing — *"Would you like me to do the 7-Day Sprint for ₹799? ₹400 now, ₹399 on handover."*
- **Cost:** ₹0 (payment link creation is free; the 1.99% fee comes out of the payment).
- **Output:** money in your account, or a clear reason why not.

**Rule:** Never run Track B before a conversation has actually happened with that person. Cold-pitching a paid service to a stranger you have not spoken to is why most beginners get 0 replies.

---

## 3. Who to talk to (target profile)

Aim for **one lane**: same city or same region helps, because you can reference local season and venues.

✅ Good fit:
- Explicitly works on weddings (posts with brides/grooms, mehendi, sangeet, wedding reels).
- 2–8 years in business, **solo or a 2–6 person studio**.
- **Visible enquiry entry point**: "DM for bookings", a website contact form, a WhatsApp button, a pricing post.
- **Evidence of active business in the last 60 days**: recent posts, "dates open for 2027", client work, story highlights of recent weddings.
- Gets a **plausible 10+ enquiries a month** — judging by follower count, comment volume, and city size. (Estimate it; don't claim it.)

❌ Poor fit (skip them):
- Huge studios with a "book a call with our team" widget and staff pages.
- Photographers who post no pricing/enquiry route at all (fully referral-based).
- Accounts with no activity in 3+ months.
- Anyone outside India (pricing, WhatsApp behaviour, and payment all differ) — non-Indian clients are a later expansion, not the pilot.

---

## 4. Discovery questions (the script)

**Rules for the conversation:**
1. Ask about **what happened last time**, not what they think in general. Past behaviour is data; opinions are noise.
2. Never ask "would you buy this?" — it produces polite lies.
3. **Do not pitch during discovery.** If they ask what you do, say: *"I'm building a small service that organises wedding enquiries and drafts the follow-ups. I'll show you at the end if you're curious — but first I want to understand your side."*
4. Take notes during or immediately after. Never record without asking.
5. Keep it to 15 minutes. Respect their time and say thank you.

**Opening message (send before the call, or use as the chat opener):**

> Hi [Name], I'm not a photographer — I'm researching how wedding photographers handle their booking enquiries and follow-ups. Would you have 10 minutes to tell me how it works on your side? Not selling anything, just trying to understand the real process. Happy to share what I learn from other photographers back with you.

### The 12 questions

| # | Question | What you are listening for |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | How long have you been shooting weddings, and roughly how many weddings a year? | Qualifies: 12+ /year is a healthy enquiry flow |
| Q2 | Where do most enquiries come from — Instagram, website, WhatsApp, referrals, wedding portals? | Channel mix; which channels get handled worst |
| Q3 | Roughly how many enquiries did you get in the last two months? | **The single most important number.** Under ~10 → likely weak fit |
| Q4 | Of those, how many would you say are still open — meaning, not booked, not rejected, not ignored? | Do they even know? "I don't know" is *the* pain signal |
| Q5 | Walk me through what happens from the moment an enquiry comes in, until a couple books or disappears. | Process visibility; where it breaks |
| Q6 | Where do the details of each enquiry live — WhatsApp, email, Instagram DMs, notes on your phone, a spreadsheet? | Scattered = opportunity; single place = weaker fit |
| Q7 | Have you ever found out later that someone enquired and you never replied, or replied very late? What happened? | A **specific story** is worth more than ten opinions. Ask: when? what happened? did they book elsewhere? |
| Q8 | When a couple receives your price list and goes quiet — what do you usually do? | Follow-up habit. "Nothing" or "I feel awkward" = strong fit |
| Q9 | How do you decide who to contact on a given day? | Nobody who says "I just remember" is actually managing it |
| Q10 | Have you ever paid for wedding leads or a portal listing? How did that go? | Budget evidence + burned-by-leads pain (the warmest prospect type) |
| Q11 | Do you use any software or tracker for this? What made you choose it (or not)? | Barrier = price, setup effort, or habit |
| Q12 | If a couple goes cold, what would make you comfortable reaching out again? | Reveals their messaging comfort zone + consent boundaries |

**Then finish with (only if the conversation went well):**

> That's really helpful. One last thing — I'm building a small pilot service: I take your existing enquiries, organise them into a tracker with a next action for each one, and write personalised follow-up drafts for you to send. 7 days, ₹799 total. I'm doing it at a low price because you'd be one of my first clients and I want honest feedback. Want me to send you the sample so you can see exactly what you'd get?

Whatever the answer, write it down and move on.

## 5. Signals: what counts as real evidence

| What they say/do | Counts as | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| "Yeah, that's a real problem." | Nothing on its own | 0 |
| "Yes, I keep forgetting to reply" | Polite | 1 |
| Names a number ("I got ~35 enquiries in Aug–Sep") | Awareness | 2 |
| Tells a specific story with a date ("a couple from Pune enquired on 3 Aug, I replied on the 19th, they'd booked someone else") | Real pain | 4 |
| Has **no idea** how many are open, and says it annoys them | Strong pain | 4 |
| Says "just send me the sample" / "show me your tracker" | Interest | 4 |
| Asks **"how much?"** or "can we start this week?" | Buying signal | 5 |
| **Pays ₹400** | Validation | 5 |
| Says "not now, after season" and doesn't reply again | Not now | 1 |
| Says "I have Studio Ninja and it's all in there" | Not a customer (for now) | 0 |

## 6. Scoring a conversation (0–5)

Give every conversation exactly one score. Log it — no memory-based scoring.

| Score | Meaning | What you do next |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | No problem acknowledged; they handle it well | Thank them. Do not follow up with an offer. |
| **1** | Vague agreement, no specifics, no urgency | One polite follow-up at day 4. Then stop. |
| **2** | Acknowledges a small annoyance ("I should reply faster, I know") | Send the free Enquiry Health Check. Stop selling. |
| **3** | Describes the problem but says now is not the time | Ask permission to check back on a **specific date** (e.g., "after your December weddings — can I message you on 20 January?"). Then actually do. |
| **4** | Describes a concrete, dated incident **and** shows interest in seeing the sample | Send the sample within 24 hours. Offer the Sprint in the same message. Follow up twice, 3 days apart, then stop. |
| **5** | Asks about price, timing, or how to pay | Send the payment link the same day. Offer to start immediately. |

**Target after day 4:** 20 conversations logged, at least 5 scoring 4–5, at least 3 offers made, at least 1 payment attempted.

## 7. Decision rules — commit to these now

| Result after 14 days | Interpretation | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| ≥5 pain cases, ≥1 payment | Problem + willingness to pay confirmed | **Continue.** Same offer, same niche. Deliver the client. Then repeat to the next 30 contacts. |
| ≥5 pain cases, 0 payments, and people say the price is fine but never pay | Interest without urgency, or trust gap | **Change the offer once.** Options, in order: (a) ask them to name their price and test it; (b) split the offer — free audit, ₹499 for the tracker only, ₹799 with drafts; (c) sell to a studio owner rather than a solo shooter; (d) shift timing to when the season slows. |
| ≥5 pain cases, 0 payments, and price/trust is clearly the block ("who are you?", "is this a scam?") | Trust problem | **Change the packaging.** Add: the sample tracker + sample report in the first message, a written agreement, a refund-in-full-if-not-delivered promise, and video-proof of you doing the work. |
| ≤4 pain cases out of 20 | The problem is **not common enough in this lane** | **Change who you sell to**, not what you sell: try photographers who have *paid for lead packages* (they demonstrably spend), then the alternative niches in `market-research.md` §10. |
| <5 replies from 40 contacts | It is a **channel** problem | Stop DMing. Move to email + website contact forms, and to communities where you participate for a week before pitching. |
| You stop contacting people for more than 3 days | Motivation problem, not a market problem | Reduce the daily quota to 5 contacts/day. Finish the week before judging anything. |

**Hard stop:** day 14. Review, decide, and either continue with a *deliberate* next 14 days or move to another niche. Never drift.

---

## 8. Ethics rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Never invent results, clients, testimonials or numbers.** "You'd be one of my first clients" is a strength, not a weakness — it justifies the low price honestly.
2. **Never say "you're losing customers."** You don't know that. Ask instead.
3. **No fake urgency.** No "limited slots" when you have none.
4. **Respect a no immediately.** One polite follow-up maximum, then stop. Never contact someone twice in a week, and never contact someone who asked you not to.
5. **Never claim to be a photographer** or to know their business better than they do.
6. **Do not record calls** without explicit permission. Do not screenshot someone's feed and post it.
7. **Personal data belongs to the client's couple.** During research you should not be collecting it at all.

## 9. Interview log

Use `interview-log.md` (created alongside this file) — copy the row template for each conversation. Fill it in **the same day**, while details are fresh.

**Weekly review questions:**
1. What is the average score of my conversations, and is it trending up?
2. Which channel produced the highest-scoring conversations?
3. What exact words did people use to describe the problem? (Use their words in my outreach — it will sound like their own language, because it is.)
4. What was the most common objection, and how will I answer it next time?
5. What did I put off doing this week because it was uncomfortable? (That task is the bottleneck — do it first tomorrow.)
